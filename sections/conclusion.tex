\section{Limitations and Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

\ours targets single-arm manipulation of task-specified, exogenously moving rigid
objects over short horizons.  It does not address language-conditioned target
selection, long-horizon task planning, deformables, or counterfactual contact
outcomes under unseen robot actions.  Dense flow after contact is scored only
under matched applied actions, and calibrated uncertainty is not itself a safety
guarantee.  Abrupt unobserved events still require re-observation and replanning.

Within this scope, the experiments establish a clear result: dynamic manipulation
improves when the policy forecasts the physical world at the time its action will
execute.  \ours combines timestamped metric 3-D history, target $\se$, dense flow,
uncertainty, a one-way physical bridge, and newest-wins execution in one
feed-forward system.  DB0 and external evaluations show that the gain persists
over matched history-aware streaming policies, while exact-current interventions
confirm that action generation causally uses the forecast.  Execution-time
alignment therefore turns metric world prediction from an auxiliary training
signal into an effective control interface.
