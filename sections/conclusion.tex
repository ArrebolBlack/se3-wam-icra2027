\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

\ours spends precision where contact depends on it: a metric SE(3) history and
forecast for the target and a sparse 3D flow for the whole scene, with the forecasts
used only as training supervision. When every method is charged its own latency, these choices let \ours act both precisely
and quickly: it outperforms reactive policies, VLAs, and world--action models, including
ones built for moving targets, on \bench and on six real-robot tasks at a p95 latency of
\fillin{XX}\,ms on \bench, and it also leads on DOMINO. \fillin{How charging latency changes the ranking relative to
paused evaluation.} \fillin{The one or two strongest ablation findings.} We will release
\bench so that future dynamic policies are evaluated with their latency charged.

\paragraph{Limitations.}
\ours requires a specified rigid target with reliable pose estimates, and its forecast
ignores how the robot's own contact changes the target's motion; learning
contact-conditioned target dynamics is a natural extension. Its target-trajectory head
predicts a single future per horizon and so cannot represent multimodal motion; an
uncertainty-aware forecast could address this. Our tasks contain no context that moves independently of the target; such
scenes would call for a motion history of the context as well, for example of its
scene flow.
