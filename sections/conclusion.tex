\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

We presented \ours, a world--action model for manipulating moving objects that
spends precision where contact depends on it and time only where it pays off. It
remembers the target's metric SE(3) motion instead of past frames, learns to
forecast the target's SE(3) trajectory precisely and the whole scene coarsely as a
sparse 3D flow, and lets its actions read a representation shaped by these forecasts
without generating them at test time. On \bench, where the scene keeps moving while a
policy computes, and on six real-robot tasks, it improves on reactive, dynamic, and
world--action policies. Five final-scale controls examine the metric motion history, future supervision, its two geometric components, and a uniform-fidelity forecast.

\paragraph{Limitations.}
\ours assumes a rigid target that moves on its own until contact, so deformable or
articulated targets and post-contact dynamics are outside its scope. It depends on a
pose estimate of the target, and tracker failures propagate to the policy; in
simulation we read the pose from the simulator. The regressed target forecast cannot
represent multimodal futures, e.g., before an impact. Our real-robot base learns
from DROID without target poses, so its pose-related parts are learned only during
fine-tuning. Finally, our tasks have a single target and slow context motion.
