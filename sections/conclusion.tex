\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

We asked what a policy needs to know, and for which instant, to act on moving
objects. An error budget in physical time points to the target's metric motion, its
SE(3) trajectory with a coarse flow of the context, and commands indexed by their
application time. \ours follows these choices and, in real time, improves on prior
policies in simulation and on a real conveyor. The paused-physics comparison shows
where the gain comes from. Large video models are strong policies when time stands
still, and they lose this edge once the scene keeps moving while they compute.
\QthreeSel{For world--action models, our results favor reading a metric forecast
when the lead is long and skipping it when the scene is static.}{For world--action
models, our results favor acting on a decoded metric forecast when the lead is
long.}{For world--action models, our results favor supervising the future at the
instants when commands apply and keeping the deployed policy fast, over generating
the future at test time.}

\paragraph{Limitations.}
The budget assumes a rigid target that moves on its own until contact, so
deformable or articulated targets and post-contact dynamics are outside its scope.
\ours depends on a pose tracker whose failures propagate to the policy. Poses from
a monocular 3D detector remove the need for an object model but cost accuracy, and
the regressed target forecast cannot represent multimodal futures, e.g.,
before an impact. Lifting DROID to 4D labels costs substantial computation.
Finally, our tasks have a single target and slow context motion, and the real-robot
study covers one task on one platform.
