\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

We asked what a policy should predict about a moving scene, and how precisely, to make
accurate contact in time. Our answer is that the requirement differs by role. The target's pose at execution
time decides contact and requires precise prediction. The surroundings require only
coarse prediction, and the static scene requires none. \ours builds this allocation
into a native 4D world--action model that acts in 15.1\,ms. It leads on \bench, on six
real-robot tasks, and on DOMINO, whose evaluator pauses during inference, so its
advantage does not come from speed alone. \bench also shows that paused evaluation overestimates slow
policies and changes the ranking of methods. \ours assumes a rigid target whose pose can be estimated online. Articulated
and deformable targets need a richer representation than one SE(3) trajectory. More broadly, our results suggest that a
world model for control should place its precision where the action is decided, instead
of spreading it uniformly over the whole scene.
