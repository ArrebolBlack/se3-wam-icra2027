\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

% We introduce \ours, a native 4D world--action model that addresses a tension at the
% core of dynamic manipulation: predicting a moving scene takes time, and that time lets
% the scene move further. By allocating precision by role, with a metric SE(3) trajectory
% for the target, sparse 3D flow for its surroundings, and the current frame for
% everything static, \ours is precise where contact is decided and acts in 15.1\,ms.
% Together with \bench, which evaluates every policy at its native latency, it offers a
% model and a benchmark for building and evaluating policies that act on time.

We presented \ours, a native 4D world--action model that allocates prediction precision by role, with precise prediction for the target, coarse prediction for its surroundings, and none for the static scene. This allocation alleviates the tension between
accurate prediction and timely action. \ours acts in 15.1\,ms and outperforms
the strongest baselines on \bench, DOMINO, and real robots. We also introduced
\bench, which evaluates every policy at its native latency. \ours currently
assumes a rigid target whose pose can be tracked online, and we leave
articulated and deformable targets to future work.
