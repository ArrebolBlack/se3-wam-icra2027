\section{Discussion and Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

Dynamic manipulation calls for a representation of the future that supports
the interaction the robot must perform. The completed study shows that adding
target and scene prediction objectives does not automatically improve a
motion-informed policy: sparse trajectories give modest point-estimate gains,
dense 2-D displacement is adverse under the tested recipe, and difficult tasks
retain shared failures. Combination and history results further show why
information access, training recipe, and execution must accompany a
representation comparison.

The evidence currently covers fourteen simulated tasks, one training seed for
the main screens, privileged target information, and synchronous execution.
The fine-target/coarse-context hypothesis, useful online foresight, multi-seed
design stability, and real-robot transfer require the specified comparisons.
Final selection will use task-specific gains and losses, action-level
interventions, and measured deployment cost.
