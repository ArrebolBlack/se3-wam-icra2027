\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

We asked what a policy should predict about a moving scene, and how precisely,
to make accurate contact in time. Our answer is that the requirement differs by
role. The target's pose at execution time decides contact and requires precise
prediction. The surroundings require only coarse prediction, and the static
scene requires none. \ours is a native 4D world--action model built on this
allocation. It is precise where contact is decided and still acts in 15.1\,ms.
It leads on \bench, on DOMINO, and on six real-robot tasks. The DOMINO evaluator
pauses during inference, so the lead there does not come from speed alone.
\bench evaluates every policy at its native latency. On the same scenes, paused
evaluation overestimates slow policies and changes the ranking of methods.
\ours assumes a rigid target whose pose can be estimated online. Articulated and
deformable targets need a richer representation than one SE(3) trajectory, and
we leave them to future work.
