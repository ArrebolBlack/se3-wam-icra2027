\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

We presented \ours, a world--action model for manipulating moving objects that
forecasts the scene for the instants at which its commands apply. It remembers the
target's metric motion, forecasts the target's SE(3) trajectory precisely and the
rest of the scene coarsely, and conditions each command on its lead. In real time,
it improves on reactive, dynamic, and world--action policies in simulation, on
DOMINO, and on a real conveyor, and a base pretrained on DROID with lifted 4D labels
carries over to the real robot. Our analysis attributes the gains to the metric
motion history and to lead-conditioned actions learned together with forecasts at
their application times, rather than to richer appearance models.
\QthreeSel{For world--action models, it favors reading a metric forecast when the
lead is long and skipping it when the scene is static.}{For world--action models,
it favors acting on a decoded metric forecast when the lead is long.}{For
world--action models, it favors supervising the future at the instants when commands
apply and keeping the deployed policy fast, over generating the future at test
time.}

\paragraph{Limitations.}
\ours assumes a rigid target that moves on its own until contact, so deformable or
articulated targets and post-contact dynamics are outside its scope. It depends on
a pose tracker whose failures propagate to the policy. Poses from a monocular 3D
detector remove the need for an object model but cost accuracy, and the regressed
target forecast cannot represent multimodal futures, e.g., before an impact.
Lifting DROID to 4D labels costs substantial computation. Finally, our tasks have a
single target and slow context motion, and the real-robot study covers one task on
one platform.
