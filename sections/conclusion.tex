\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

\ours spends precision where contact depends on it: a metric SE(3) history and
forecast for the target and a sparse 3D flow for the whole scene, with the forecasts
used only as training supervision. When every method is charged its own latency, these choices let \ours act both precisely
and quickly: it outperforms reactive policies, VLAs, and world--action models, including
ones built for moving targets, on \bench and on six real-robot tasks at a p95 latency of
15.1\,ms on \bench with compiled BF16 inference. It also leads in overall SR on the DOMINO W5 split.
The strongest baseline is PUMA under native latency and $\pi_{0.5}$+SE(3) in the
paused column. At native latency on \bench, removing target pose history and
removing future supervision reduce success by 19.6 and 4.3 percentage points,
respectively. We will release \bench so that future dynamic policies are evaluated
with their latency charged.

\paragraph{Limitations.}
Deployment requires a specified rigid target and reliable online pose estimates.
The target forecast predicts one future per horizon and ignores the effects of robot
contact on target motion. Uncertainty-aware forecasts and contact-conditioned dynamics
could address these limits. Independently moving context lies outside our task set and
would need its own motion history, for example from scene flow.
