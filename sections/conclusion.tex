\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

\ours studies dynamic manipulation through the information needed at
interaction time. The model connects timestamped history, structured target
dynamics, scene prediction, and action generation within explicit training
and deployment interfaces. Its benchmark and experiments separate
information quality, auxiliary supervision, online forecast use, and
computation cost. The diagnostic evidence shows why these distinctions
matter: a readable history need not improve control, and future-supervision
benefits can depend strongly on the surrounding representation.
The complete evaluation tests these design choices through matched
confirmation, robot-data pretraining, and independent downstream adaptation.
